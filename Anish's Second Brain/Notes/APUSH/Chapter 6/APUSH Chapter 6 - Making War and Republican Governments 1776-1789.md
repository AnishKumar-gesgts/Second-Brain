> **Quiz focus:** How did the Patriots win a war against a stronger empire, and why did the weaknesses of the first republican governments lead to the Constitution?

## The chapter in one chain

Britain's army and navy were much stronger, but it could not destroy the Continental Army or sustain control over a large, hostile population. Saratoga brought France into the war, French naval support helped trap Cornwallis at Yorktown, and the Treaty of Paris recognized independence. Meanwhile, wartime inflation, debt, interstate conflict, and Shays's Rebellion exposed the Articles of Confederation's weakness. Nationalists therefore drafted the Constitution, which Federalists defended as a stronger but still republican national government.

## 1. Winning the War for Independence

### Why Britain began with major advantages

- Britain had about 11 million people to the colonies' 2.5 million, access to imperial wealth, the world's strongest navy, a standing army, German **Hessian** soldiers, Loyalists, and several Native American allies.
- The Continental Army was small, inexperienced, poorly supplied, and backed by a Congress that could not reliably tax. Many soldiers deserted rather than accept long military service.
- Britain aimed first to seize New York and the Hudson River, isolating radical New England from the middle and southern colonies.

### Why the Patriots survived

- **Washington's strategy:** avoid a decisive destruction of his army; stretch British supply lines and wear down British morale. Keeping the army alive kept the Revolution alive.
- **Local support:** Patriots controlled most local governments, raised militias, and obtained men, food, and supplies through community networks.
- **British limits:** General William Howe hoped for a political settlement and did not annihilate Washington's army when he had opportunities. Britain also had to control a huge area far from home.
- **Trenton and Princeton:** Washington's surprise attacks in late 1776 and early 1777 were small militarily but restored morale after defeats around New York.

### Saratoga and the international turn

- In 1777, British General **John Burgoyne** moved south from Canada toward Albany. His plan failed when American forces, led by **Horatio Gates**, defeated him at Saratoga.
- **Saratoga was the turning point** because it convinced France that the Patriots could win. France entered as a formal ally in 1778, bringing money, weapons, troops, and naval strength. Spain later joined the wider war against Britain.
- Britain could no longer concentrate solely on North America; it had to defend Atlantic, Caribbean, and Indian Ocean interests against European enemies.

### Wartime hardship and finance

- States and Congress issued huge quantities of paper money because they feared taxation and lacked dependable revenue. Overissue caused severe **inflation**; Continental currency rapidly lost value.
- A **currency tax** was the hidden burden inflation placed on ordinary people: money they accepted for goods, supplies, or wages lost purchasing power before they could spend it.
- Shortages, price spikes, and unpaid soldiers weakened morale. State price controls usually failed because farmers and merchants avoided selling at artificially low prices.
- At **Valley Forge**, Washington's army endured the winter of 1777-1778 with hunger, illness, inadequate shelter, and clothing shortages. **Baron von Steuben** imposed drill and discipline, making the smaller force more effective.

### The South and Yorktown

- Britain shifted south after 1778, expecting Loyalist support and using the **Philipsburg Proclamation** (1779), which offered freedom to enslaved people who escaped Patriot masters and joined British forces.
- The British won Charleston and Camden, but southern campaigns drained their resources. Patriot regulars and militia, including forces associated with Nathanael Greene, kept fighting a war of attrition.
- In 1781, French naval power blocked British escape by sea at **Yorktown, Virginia**. Washington's combined American-French army forced Lord **Cornwallis** to surrender.
- **Cause/effect:** Saratoga -> French alliance -> French naval and military assistance -> Yorktown -> British willingness to negotiate peace.

### Peace settlement

- The **Treaty of Paris of 1783** recognized the United States as independent and set its western boundary at the Mississippi River. Americans gained the most from the treaty; France and Spain gained far less than they hoped.

## 2. The Revolution changed politics - but unevenly

### State constitutions and republicanism

- Independence turned colonists from subjects of a king into citizens who claimed a role in self-government.
- Between 1776 and 1781, states wrote constitutions based on popular sovereignty, elected legislatures, and written limits on government. Most kept property qualifications for voting, so political equality remained limited.
- The **Pennsylvania constitution of 1776** was especially democratic: a single-house legislature, broad male suffrage, annual elections, and a weak executive. Critics feared it gave too much power to a temporary majority.
- A **mixed government** balances social interests through separate branches and checks; many leaders preferred it to Pennsylvania's dominant legislature.

### Women, Loyalists, Native Americans, and enslaved people

- Women performed farmwork, produced cloth and supplies, organized protests, and made political claims. **Judith Sargent Murray** argued in "On the Equality of the Sexes" that women had equal intellectual ability.
- The Revolution did not give women the vote. The emerging ideal of **republican motherhood** valued women mainly as educators of virtuous republican sons.
- Loyalists lost property, faced intimidation or exile, and were often politically excluded after the war.
- Many Native nations allied with Britain because U.S. expansion threatened their homelands. The Treaty of Paris ignored Native sovereignty and handed the United States land Native peoples still occupied.
- Enslaved people sought freedom by joining either army. Northern states began gradual emancipation, but slavery continued and expanded in the South. Revolutionary language about liberty therefore challenged slavery without ending it.

## 3. The Articles of Confederation: achievements and failures

- The **Articles of Confederation** created a "firm league of friendship" among sovereign states. Congress could conduct diplomacy, make war, and manage western lands, but could not levy taxes, regulate interstate or foreign commerce, or force states to obey national decisions.
- Each state had one vote in Congress; major changes required broad agreement. There was no independent executive and no national judiciary.
- **Achievement - western land:** states ceded competing western claims to Congress, creating a national domain. The **Northwest Ordinance of 1787** established a process for territories to become equal states, banned slavery in the Northwest Territory, and encouraged public education.
- **Failure - economic conflict:** states issued paper money, set their own trade policies, and imposed barriers on one another. Congress could request money but not compel it, so it struggled with war debt and national defense.

## 4. Shays's Rebellion and the Constitution

### Why Shays's Rebellion mattered

- Postwar Massachusetts demanded hard-money taxes to pay debts. Many western farmers, already facing debt and foreclosures, wanted relief and paper currency.
- In 1786-1787, **Daniel Shays** and other farmers closed courts to stop debt collections. Massachusetts eventually used a privately funded force to defeat them.
- The rebellion showed nationalists that state governments could be unstable and that the Confederation could not respond effectively to a domestic crisis. It pushed more leaders toward constitutional reform.

### Philadelphia Convention compromises

- Delegates met in 1787 intending to revise the Articles but instead designed a new Constitution.
- The **Virginia Plan**, associated with James Madison, proposed representation based on population, favoring large states.
- The **New Jersey Plan** called for equal state representation, protecting small states.
- The Great Compromise created a bicameral Congress: House representation by population and equal representation in the Senate.
- The Constitution created a stronger federal government with power to tax, regulate commerce, raise armies, enforce laws, and use a separate executive and judiciary. It also barred states from issuing paper money or impairing contracts.
- The Three-Fifths Compromise counted three-fifths of enslaved people for representation and taxation, increasing slave states' political power while treating enslaved people as property.

### Federalists vs. Antifederalists

| Group | Main argument | Key concern / response |
|---|---|---|
| **Federalists** | A stronger national government was necessary for security, stable commerce, credit, and order. | Madison's **Federalist No. 10** argued that a large republic with many competing factions makes it harder for one majority faction to dominate. |
| **Antifederalists** | A distant, powerful government threatened the Revolution's promise of local self-rule. | They feared high taxes, a standing army, an overpowered president, and rule by wealthy elites; they demanded a Bill of Rights. |

- Ratification required conventions in nine states, not unanimous state-legislature approval. Close votes in Massachusetts, Virginia, and New York were secured partly by the promise to add a **Bill of Rights**.

## Short-answer practice

1. **Why did Britain lose despite its advantages?**
   Britain could win battles but could not destroy Washington's army or control a vast hostile population. Patriot local support sustained the war, Washington avoided a fatal defeat, and Saratoga brought France into the conflict. French naval support then made Yorktown possible.

2. **How did war finance affect ordinary Americans and politics?**
   States and Congress printed paper money because they lacked reliable tax revenue. Inflation acted as a currency tax on soldiers, suppliers, and consumers, producing shortages and protests. The disorder strengthened arguments that the nation needed more effective fiscal power.

3. **How revolutionary was the American Revolution?**
   It replaced monarchy with written republican governments and expanded political participation among many white men. However, property requirements persisted, women remained disenfranchised, Native land claims were ignored, and slavery survived despite northern emancipation measures. Its egalitarian language therefore created challenges to hierarchy more than immediate equality.

4. **Why did the Articles give way to the Constitution?**
   The Articles succeeded at western land policy but could not tax, regulate trade, enforce national decisions, or respond to economic conflict. Shays's Rebellion made the danger of this weakness vivid. The Constitution answered with federal taxing, commerce, military, executive, and judicial powers while preserving state representation.

## Last-minute memory hooks

- **Saratoga -> France -> Yorktown -> Independence.**
- **Articles: land success, money-and-power failure.** Congress could organize the West, but not reliably tax, trade-regulate, or enforce.
- **Shays -> stronger Constitution.** Debt crisis and court closings convinced nationalists that the Confederation was too weak.
- **Federalists = extended republic; Antifederalists = local liberty + Bill of Rights.**
