# Publication-Potential Assessment

Reviewed 2026-09-07 after completing the first two adapters and the exact
four-mode Bell-measurement propagation study.

## Verdict

**The project is scientifically valid and produces useful results, but the
current evidence does not justify continued solo pursuit with a top-journal
publication as the expected outcome.**

The compiler should be retained as working research infrastructure. A focused
methods or specialist-journal paper could become realistic after independent
validation and one native architecture study. However, a top-journal paper would
require a substantially larger result: a realistic boosted, encoded, or
repeat-until-success architecture whose threshold, subthreshold overhead, or
hardware ranking changes because of the compiled physical noise.

That result is possible, but the present evidence does not show that it is
likely. It therefore cannot be promised or treated as the expected continuation.

## Evidence supporting the project

1. The compiler contract works across two distinct physical mechanisms without
   mechanism-specific changes to its basic event/visibility separation.
2. The second adapter identified a real detector-aliasing effect. A valid source
   herald concealed vacuum or multiphoton output in 1.0194%-18.5692% of the
   source-level conditions tested.
3. Exact propagation through a standard four-mode Bell measurement showed that
   some multiphoton-plus-loss histories survive photon-number-resolving checks
   as apparently valid full Bell records.
4. The full-success remote states were Bell-diagonal to at worst
   $1.32\times10^{-17}$ off-diagonal magnitude, so the extracted fusion-bit
   errors did not require an added Pauli-twirling approximation.

## Evidence against a top-journal claim now

1. The first adapter produced a large channel-distribution difference but no
   significant logical difference in 18 held-out comparisons.
2. In the second adapter, hidden error among valid full Bell records ranged from
   0.00036% to 1.7528%. This can matter near a fault-tolerance boundary, but the
   effect itself is not universally large.
3. Multiphoton-induced false Bell records are already recognized experimentally;
   the novelty cannot be the existence of the mechanism. Hauser et al. report
   multiphoton emission as a limitation on Bell-measurement discrimination, and
   Randles et al. explicitly model heralded sources with multiphoton errors.
4. The unboosted Bell measurement has an intrinsic 50% failure probability. In
   the high-quality frozen condition
   ($\mu=0.01$, $\eta_s=\eta_{id}=\eta_d=0.99$), the exact model still has
   48.01% partial failure; source and detector loss add 3.98% wrong-count
   rejection. This is not a useful native unencoded six-ring operating point.
5. Current FBQC work has moved beyond threshold-only demonstrations toward
   realistic subthreshold overhead. Löbl et al. show that fusion failure creates
   a logical-error floor and that all-linear-optical schemes can require orders
   of magnitude more resources than emitter-based repeat-until-success designs.
6. A decisive continuation would need a validated resource-state generator,
   boosted or encoded fusion, native geometry, and subthreshold overhead study.
   That is a major new modeling program and would benefit strongly from expert
   supervision or collaboration.

## What would change the verdict

Resume the top-journal version only if at least one of these becomes available:

- collaboration with a photonic-QEC group that can validate the resource-state
  and encoded-fusion mapping;
- experimental click-pattern data that the compiler predicts more accurately
  than an existing aggregate model;
- a tractable boosted or repeat-until-success primitive for which the compiled
  hidden errors reverse a hardware ranking or materially change subthreshold
  overhead;
- evidence that the same compiler predicts a decision-changing logical effect
  in a third mechanism without tuning the hypothesis around a favorable corner.

## Recommended disposition

Pause active expansion here. Preserve the code, exact event tables, tests, and
negative results. The work is a credible foundation for collaboration or a
specialist paper, but continuing alone specifically for a top-journal outcome
would have a low expected return relative to the required scope.
